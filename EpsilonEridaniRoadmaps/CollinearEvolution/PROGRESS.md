## CollinearEvolution: 2026-06-10 to 2026-11-02 (`1c3cef5` to `f8d9db7`)

The first work on this roadmap is all in its pure-mathematics foundation, and the headline is the [quasi-shuffle relation](https://eic.github.io/EpsilonEridani/docs/EpsilonEridani/Mathematics/HarmonicSum/QuasiShuffle.html#EpsilonEridani.harmonicSum_mul_harmonicSum) for nested harmonic sums: the product of two sums with the same argument is the sum indexed by the [quasi-shuffle product](https://eic.github.io/EpsilonEridani/docs/EpsilonEridani/Mathematics/HarmonicSum/QuasiShuffle.html#EpsilonEridani.HarmonicSum.quasiShuffle) of their multi-indices. It covers alternating sums too, and gives the roadmap's check [S₁² = 2S₁,₁ − S₂](https://eic.github.io/EpsilonEridani/docs/EpsilonEridani/Mathematics/HarmonicSum/QuasiShuffle.html#EpsilonEridani.harmonicSum_singleton_one_mul_self) for every N (EpsilonEridani#67). The first harmonic sum now also has a [continuation to complex argument](https://eic.github.io/EpsilonEridani/docs/EpsilonEridani/Mathematics/HarmonicSum/Continuation.html#EpsilonEridani.complexHarmonic), built as ψ(s+1) + γ. It is meromorphic, satisfies the recurrence and the [reflection formula](https://eic.github.io/EpsilonEridani/docs/EpsilonEridani/Mathematics/HarmonicSum/Continuation.html#EpsilonEridani.complexHarmonic_neg), and has a [simple pole of residue −1](https://eic.github.io/EpsilonEridani/docs/EpsilonEridani/Mathematics/HarmonicSum/Continuation.html#EpsilonEridani.tendsto_mul_complexHarmonic_neg_nat_succ) at each negative integer. Large-N asymptotics are proved to second order for [S₁](https://eic.github.io/EpsilonEridani/docs/EpsilonEridani/Mathematics/HarmonicSum/Asymptotics.html#EpsilonEridani.isBigO_harmonic_sub_log_sub_eulerMascheroniConstant_sub_inv_two_mul_inv_sq) and for [the digamma function](https://eic.github.io/EpsilonEridani/docs/EpsilonEridani/Mathematics/SpecialFunctions/Gamma/Digamma/Asymptotics.html#EpsilonEridani.isBigO_digamma_natCast_sub_log_add_inv_two_mul_inv_sq) along the integers. Still to come: uniqueness of the continuation, higher-weight sums and polylogarithms.

On the interval itself there is now a [Mellin convolution](https://eic.github.io/EpsilonEridani/docs/EpsilonEridani/QFT/Factorization/Convolution/Algebra.html#EpsilonEridani.QFT.Factorization.Convolution.mellinConv) that is bilinear, commutative and [positivity-preserving](https://eic.github.io/EpsilonEridani/docs/EpsilonEridani/QFT/Factorization/Convolution/Algebra.html#EpsilonEridani.QFT.Factorization.Convolution.mellinConv_apply_nonneg), and its moments are [products of moments](https://eic.github.io/EpsilonEridani/docs/EpsilonEridani/QFT/Factorization/Convolution/Algebra.html#EpsilonEridani.QFT.Factorization.Convolution.mellinDis_mellinConv). It also has a proof that [no function is its unit](https://eic.github.io/EpsilonEridani/docs/EpsilonEridani/QFT/Factorization/Convolution/Algebra.html#EpsilonEridani.QFT.Factorization.Convolution.not_mellinConv_indicator_one_ae_eq), not even almost everywhere. Associativity and the δ(1−x) unit are not yet there. The [plus functional](https://eic.github.io/EpsilonEridani/docs/EpsilonEridani/Mathematics/Distribution/PlusFunctional.html#EpsilonEridani.plusFunctional) [K]₊ comes with its convergence theorem and the multiplication identity that puts kernels into normal form. Its standard case [1/(1−z)]₊ kills constants and [has the negative harmonic numbers as its moments](https://eic.github.io/EpsilonEridani/docs/EpsilonEridani/Mathematics/Distribution/PlusFunctional.html#EpsilonEridani.plusOneMinus_pow), which is the roadmap's first acceptance example. Convergence is proved for test functions that are Lipschitz at 1, not for arbitrary continuous ones, and the uniqueness of the normal form is still open.
